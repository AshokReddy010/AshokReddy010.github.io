A daily briefing on New York City's 311 service requests from NYC Open Data: yesterday's volume against a typical day, the complaint types furthest above normal for that weekday, a map of every request, two years of weekly trends, and how fast each agency closes requests. A scheduled workflow refreshes it every morning.

How this works

Data. NYC Open Data's 311 Service Requests (2020 to present), queried every morning through the Socrata API. Daily counts per complaint type and borough are aggregated on the server, so only small tables travel.

Late arrivals. Requests keep arriving for days, so the last ten days are re-pulled on every run, and a day that is still far below its usual volume is not shown as the headline.

Unusual. A robust z-score against the same weekday over the previous eight weeks (median and median absolute deviation). Types with fewer than 15 requests on a typical day are left out.

Closure. Measured on requests at least eight days old, so "within a week" is final. Closed dates earlier than the created date, a known data artefact, are treated as unknown. "Closed" means the agency closed the request, not necessarily that the problem was fixed.

Case notes

311 has a strong weekly rhythm and records keep arriving for days, so naive day-over-day comparisons cry wolf.

Approach

  • Aggregate at the source: daily counts come from the Socrata API's group-by, so two years arrive as small tables.
  • Re-pull the last ten days on every run, and skip a headline day that is still filling in.
  • Compare each day with the same weekday over eight weeks; rank only rises, since recent drops are usually late data.
  • Measure closure only on requests at least eight days old, treating impossible closed dates as unknown.
