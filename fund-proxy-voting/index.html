Do ESG Funds Vote Like ESG Funds?

I collected three years of fund voting records from the SEC, 73.8 million votes from 12,416 funds, to test whether funds with ESG in their name vote differently from ordinary funds. They do, a little: the fund house a fund belongs to predicts its votes better than its label.

Do ESG funds vote like ESG funds?

Mostly not. Across three years of shareholder votes, the fund house a fund belongs to says more about how it votes than the word "ESG" in its name.

How it was built

Data. Every US fund files Form N-PX with the SEC each year listing every vote it cast. A restartable downloader collected 33,929 filings and 73.8 million votes for the reporting years ending June 2024, 2025 and 2026.

Cleaning. dbt models in DuckDB standardise more than 40 spellings of ten vote answers and 12 date formats; 99.97% of votes are recognised.

Matching. Each filer words the same proposal differently, so 8,274 wordings were matched into 3,850 proposals.

Direction. A proposal "supports ESG" when specialist ESG houses backed it, with keyword rules as a fallback. An LLM labeller was also tested against a hand-labelled set: 91.0% accuracy on 578 held-out proposals.

Case notes

Funds marketed as ESG or sustainable are expected to back environmental and social proposals at shareholder meetings. Every US fund must publish its votes, but the records are spread across tens of thousands of inconsistent filings, so the claim is hard to check at scale.

Approach

  • Collect: a restartable downloader read 33,929 SEC filings and saved 73.8 million votes.
  • Clean: dbt models standardise more than 40 spellings of ten answers and 12 date formats, with 99.97% of votes recognised.
  • Match: the same proposal is worded differently by each filer, so 8,274 wordings were matched into 3,850 proposals.
  • Label: each proposal's direction comes from how specialist ESG fund houses voted, with keyword rules as a fallback.

What I found

  • Label: on proposals that specialist ESG houses backed, ESG-named funds elsewhere voted for 33% in 2026, against 14% for other funds and 92% for the specialists.
  • Trend: ordinary funds' support halved in two years, from 28% to 14%.
  • Siblings: on the same proposal, an ESG-named fund voted the same way as ordinary funds from its own filer 83% to 87% of the time.
  • Range: of 145 ESG-named funds, 41 backed under 10% of these proposals and 21 backed 75% or more.

Testing a language model

  • Question: could an LLM label a proposal's direction from its title alone? I scored it against the specialist houses' votes on 815 proposals, split into a practice set and a final set that was scored once.
  • Prompt: the same model rose from 69% to 85% accuracy across three prompts. Asking how specialist funds would vote worked better than asking whether a proposal supports ESG.
  • Combined: keyword rules first, then the model where the rules gave no answer, reached 91.0% on the 578 final proposals. It still misses about a quarter of opposing proposals.

What made it hard

  • Misleading column: a field named for management's recommendation records whether the vote went with or against it. Read correctly, funds vote with management 89.9% of the time.
  • Misleading titles: proposals such as "Revisit Plastics Packaging Policies" sound pro-ESG but received 0% from specialist houses. Keyword rules alone agreed with those votes 91% of the time.
  • Limits: voting is one measure of a fund, the ESG label is read from fund names, and the yardstick is a chosen group of ten fund houses.
